# The Architecture of Apotheosis

*The aesthetic doctrine of project invocations, sovereign desire, poetic release, and ordered excess beneath the Second
Sun*

> **An invocation need not reveal what the project does.  
> It should reveal what the project dreams of being.**

> **The question is not always whether the desire is permissible.  
> Sometimes the question is whether heaven possesses sufficient force to stop it.**

---

## Operating Assumption

This document defines the art, not who must perform each stage.

The corpus recognizes three operating forms, in descending order of priority:

1. **Human-reviewed, agent-written, agent-orchestrated** — the default. Agents generate, develop, criticize,
   bind, and revise the work. A human reviews the principal creative selections and alone grants final adoption.
2. **Fully agent** — agents perform generation, orchestration, independent selection, criticism, revision, and
   adoption.
3. **Fully human** — one human performs the same artistic stages directly.

In the primary form, the human is neither the writer nor the Operator. The human is the aesthetic authority at the
principal narrowing points:

- desire selection;
- cultural inheritance selection;
- premise selection;
- invocation or spine selection;
- final adoption.

The Agent Operator handles all orchestration around those gates: context isolation, packet construction, specialist
selection, candidate curation, criticism, integration, late binding, and poetic revision.

> **The agents make the possibilities.  
> The human decides which possibility earns another form.**

The exact process is defined in [The Conclave of Apotheosis](CONCLAVE.md). A compact practical path is available
in [A Short Walkthrough for ChatGPT.com](WALKTHROUGH.md).

## 1. Purpose

A project invocation is a ceremonial work attached to a project’s identity.

It may appear near the beginning of a README, documentation site, release announcement, trailer, banner, or other
defining artifact. It may resemble an aria, prayer, oath, creed, spell, covenant, activation sequence, confession,
summons, or declaration of a True Name.

It does not need to explain the project.

It does not need to encode the project’s architecture in metaphor.

It does not need to permit a reader to reconstruct the feature list by decoding its symbols.

It needs to **belong**.

That belonging may arise from:

- the project’s purpose;
- its name or etymology;
- its kanji;
- its banner and visual identity;
- one emotional or philosophical affinity;
- a specific inherited myth, rite, liturgy, theater, poetic form, court art, or craft tradition;
- an invented character, event, or legend adopted by the project;
- a private association held by the author;
- meaning accumulated through repeated use.

The connection may be direct.

It may be oblique.

It may begin as little more than aesthetic recognition.

The invocation and project are allowed to teach the reader how they belong together.

The ordinary README shall remain plain, exact, and useful. Installation instructions shall not become rituals.
Limitations shall not become prophecies. API behavior shall not be hidden beneath metaphor.

The invocation stands apart from that prose.

It is the threshold before the workshop.

## 2. The First Canon

An invocation must possess a life beyond the feature it accompanies.

It may dramatize a technical truth, but it need not do so. It may instead give the project an unreal identity: a private
mythology, impossible aspiration, forbidden world, aesthetic law, or soul it has not yet become.

The project supplies a place in which the rite may take root.

The rite supplies a destiny the implementation alone could never contain.

> **Belonging is required. Explanation is not.**

---

## 3. Credo, Logion, Parable, and Invocation

These forms may stand beside one another. They perform different acts.

### 3.1 Credo

A credo states what is believed.

> The written word must remain answerable to execution.

### 3.2 Logion

A logion reveals a truth.

> A name that cannot restore the face it mourns is only a second grave.

### 3.3 Parable

A parable places a principle or desire inside a narrative intended to reveal a lesson.

> A king demanded perfect memory and learned that a kingdom unable to forget could never forgive.

A parable may be beautiful.

It is not automatically an invocation.

### 3.4 Invocation

An invocation makes something happen.

> Therefore let the grave become a gate.  
> Let the sealed name recover its voice.  
> Let the road of return be opened—

The distinction is not merely length or volume.

> **A credo professes.  
> A logion reveals.  
> A parable teaches.  
> An invocation activates.**

An invocation must possess movement. Something is opened, summoned, transformed, judged, bound, relinquished, enthroned,
conquered, awakened, named, or released.

A philosophical monologue may be beautiful.

Without movement, it remains a monologue.

## 4. Belonging Before Explanation

A project should provide a gravitational field, not necessarily a specification.

The relationship between project and invocation may take three broad forms. These names belong in the workshop, not
above the finished rite.

### 4.1 The Mirror

The invocation closely reflects the project’s purpose or governing principle.

```text
project truth
    ↓
human or mythic analogue
    ↓
invocation
```

A project about preserving knowledge through transformation might inspire a rite about someone whose former name
continues to govern them after genuine change.

A project about executable documentation might inspire a rite in which written testimony must rise and answer before a
living witness.

**Strengths**

- The connection can become satisfying once recognized.
- Technical and aesthetic identities reinforce one another.
- The invocation can dramatize the project’s actual thesis.

**Dangers**

- Documentation in robes.
- One-to-one allegory.
- Feature lists rewritten as liturgy.
- A release that merely paraphrases the package description.

The Mirror is valid.

It is not the default merely because it is easiest to justify.

### 4.2 The Echo

The invocation shares one meaningful thread with the project but tells its own story.

That thread may be a dream, hidden relation, witness, inheritance, translation, transformation, memory, recursion, false
awakening, or boundary between incompatible worlds.

Everything else may be invented freely.

A project called Yumemi may adopt a rite about an artist who saw one impossible face in dream and spends a lifetime
carving the waking world into submission before it.

No unit, equation, or act of measurement need appear.

**Strengths**

- Creative freedom without complete arbitrariness.
- The invocation can function as fiction before functioning as project art.
- The project gains associations beyond its implementation.

**Dangers**

- The shared thread may become too faint.
- The writer may compensate by explaining the symbolism afterward.
- The result may become generic surreal fantasy.

### 4.3 The Adoption

The invocation is nearly autonomous.

It belongs because the project claims it.

The project may adopt:

- the final prayer of an invented saint;
- a forbidden machine protocol;
- the creed of a fictional order;
- a dream-world whose laws remain unexplained;
- a character’s ultimate technique;
- a myth written solely for the project.

The association may begin arbitrarily and acquire meaning through repetition.

**Strengths**

- Maximum creative freedom.
- The project may acquire lore rather than merely illustrate itself.
- The invocation can surprise.
- Long use may make the adopted myth inseparable from the project.

**Dangers**

- The rite may feel pasted on.
- Generic dark-fantasy prose can be adopted by anything.
- Randomness may be mistaken for mystery.

Adoption succeeds when the rite has enough identity that the project can make it its own.

### 4.4 Late Binding

A living invocation may be bound to the project after its premise and voice are already complete.

This is not a compromise.

It is often the strongest method.

Binding may require only:

- one image;
- one dream;
- one line;
- one visual motif;
- one release component;
- one etymological echo.

Do not distribute project symbolism evenly.

One charged line may be sufficient:

> Before birth clothed me in this lesser face,  
> I had already seen the other in dream.

Do not rebuild a living rite merely to justify its repository.

### 4.5 The Two Tests

Remove the project title.

Ask:

> Does the invocation possess a life of its own?

Restore the project context.

Ask:

> Does this feel like something the project could rightfully claim?

Recognition is enough.

An explanatory paragraph is not required.

## 5. The Doctrine Governs Form, Not Content

The Doctrine of the Second Sun supplies:

- discipline;
- proportion;
- hierarchy;
- symbolic coherence;
- technical craftsmanship;
- respect for inherited words;
- seriousness toward the work;
- excess given deliberate form.

It does not impose a metaphysics tax.

An invocation beneath the Doctrine does not need to:

- praise order;
- condemn chaos;
- depict conversion;
- submit desire to a higher law;
- endorse restoration;
- mention the Second Sun;
- agree morally with the Doctrine;
- speak in the Doctrine’s own voice.

The speaker may be heroic, monstrous, deluded, devotional, rebellious, nihilistic, romantic, erotic, imperial, ecstatic,
wrathful, playful, or unresolved.

A private obsession may become a world.

A higher law may judge the speaker.

A machine may activate without psychology.

A sincere desire may remain sincere.

A monstrous desire may win.

> **The rite must possess order.  
> It need not make order its subject.**

### 5.1 Ordered Excess

The governing principle is:

> **Human truth disciplined into coherent excess.**

The Doctrine ensures that:

- every image belongs;
- every repetition advances;
- every escalation has a cause;
- every grand claim descends from the animating desire;
- every line prepares the release.

The Doctrine does not remove spectacle.

It gives spectacle a form capable of carrying weight.

Chaos may supply the cry.

It may not supply the editing.

## 6. Desire Is Not a Therapy Patient

Machine writers often interpret “emotionally dangerous” through one narrow structure:

```text
vulnerable person
→ respectable need
→ delicate symbolic image
→ cruel ironic consequence
→ tragic ethical decision
→ moral lesson
```

This form is permitted.

Its monopoly is not.

An ultimate invocation is not obligated to place desire before a therapeutic tribunal.

### 6.1 Danger Is Not Victimhood

Danger may arise from:

- pride;
- ambition;
- rivalry;
- conquest;
- erotic fixation;
- impossible standards of beauty or craft;
- aristocratic contempt;
- hatred given metaphysical consistency;
- loyalty that refuses death;
- hunger for recognition;
- desire for supremacy;
- ecstatic self-deification;
- willingness to make everyone inhabit one private truth.

A wounded person may possess a powerful rite.

So may a king, rival, knight, artist, craftsman, machine, conqueror, or disappointed god.

### 6.2 Desire Need Not Be Respectable

The speaker may desire:

- worship;
- supremacy;
- one worthy enemy;
- the sole standard of beauty;
- preservation of one beloved person at the expense of worlds;
- recognition from heaven;
- conquest for its own sake;
- one perfect work;
- love made sovereign;
- transformation beyond every category.

The desire need not be endorsed.

It must possess specific internal logic.

### 6.3 The Universe Is Not Automatically a Moral Instructor

Do not automatically punish desire through ironic law.

Sometimes:

- the speaker wins;
- the desire becomes law exactly as intended;
- the victory is beautiful;
- the victory is horrifying;
- the speaker considers the distinction irrelevant.

### 6.4 Victory Is Allowed

An ultimate release may be a genuine victory.

The speaker may ascend, conquer, complete the work, defeat the rival, preserve the beloved, overthrow the law, become
the law, or force creation to acknowledge them.

Do not convert every triumph into:

> But at what cost?

The speaker may know the cost.

They may celebrate it.

They may consider it irrelevant.

They may force others to pay.

### 6.5 Price Is Not Punishment

Distinguish:

- **opposition:** what resists the desire;
- **difficulty:** what makes victory hard;
- **price:** what must be spent;
- **sacrifice:** what the speaker willingly relinquishes;
- **collateral:** what others lose;
- **limitation:** what the power cannot do;
- **punishment:** what condemns the desire;
- **irony:** the desire defeating itself.

A price does not prove the desire was wrong.

Self-erasure may be completion.

Sacrifice may prove commitment.

Collateral may accompany a real victory.

### 6.6 Sympathy Is Optional

The reader does not need to approve of the speaker.

The reader should feel some combination of awe, dread, admiration, fascination, attraction, horror, exhilaration, or the
force of a will that has become difficult to dismiss.

Majesty may precede sympathy.

A villain’s rite may be stronger because it never asks to be forgiven.

### 6.7 The Speaker May Know Exactly What They Are Doing

Do not make every caster reluctant, tearful, morally conflicted, surprised by the price, forced into the release, or
horrified when it works.

A speaker may have waited their entire life for permission to proceed.

They may smile when heaven finally understands the scale of its error.

### 6.8 The Rite Need Not Teach

An invocation is not obligated to deliver a lesson about trauma, moderation, grief, ambition, or letting go.

It may reveal such things.

It may also reject them.

Some desires deserve judgment.

The invocation is not always the court required to deliver it.

## 7. Desire Before Backstory

The first creative question should not be:

> What terrible thing happened to this person?

It should be:

> What do they want so absolutely that reality itself becomes the obstacle?

Backstory may explain the desire.

It should not automatically create it.

### 7.1 Desire Families

Possible engines include:

- **Sovereignty:** recognition, supremacy, self-coronation, worship.
- **Conquest and rivalry:** the worthy enemy, the final horizon, victory beyond history.
- **Beauty and craft:** one perfect work, the defeat of nature, mastery beyond inheritance.
- **Recognition and permanence:** one name beyond death, one moment beyond time.
- **Loyalty and love:** devotion beyond distance, identity, reincarnation, or law.
- **Ecstasy and transformation:** divinity, apotheosis, forbidden sensation, a form beyond category.
- **Rebellion and hatred:** refusal made permanent, the eternal law broken because it called itself eternal.
- **Fellowship and succession:** companions gathered beneath one dream, a lineage greater than its founder.

These are not templates.

They demonstrate that emotional force need not begin with victimhood.

### 7.2 Victory Before Cost

Determine:

1. desire;
2. claim;
3. complete victory;
4. manifested law;

before determining:

5. opposition;
6. price;
7. stance toward price.

When cost is invented first, the premise often becomes a parable.

When victory is imagined first, the premise acquires will.

### 7.3 The Sovereign Premise

A complete premise contains:

- **Desire:** what the speaker wants.
- **Claim:** why reality owes it.
- **Opposition:** what prevents it.
- **Victory:** what complete success looks like.
- **Manifested law:** how reality operates afterward.
- **Stance toward price:** what the speaker thinks of the cost.
- **Recurring image:** one image native to the desire.
- **Final act:** the irreversible release.
- **Release concept:** the proper name of that act.

### 7.4 Cultural Inheritance Before Premise

Agent-written invocations often become culturally orphaned when they invent every layer at once:

- civilization;
- religion;
- ritual office;
- costume;
- magical mechanism;
- ceremonial object;
- metaphysical vocabulary;
- release name.

The result may be novel and still feel weightless. A salt city, porcelain mask, dream prophet, or impossible theater
has no inherited gravity merely because it is strange.

For substantial agent-written rites, the normal sequence is:

```text
desire
    ↓
selected cultural inheritance
    ↓
premise
    ↓
invocation
```

A cultural inheritance is not “Western flavor,” “Japanese flavor,” incense, masks, Latin, shrines, cathedrals, or
courtly vocabulary. It is a specific inherited form whose structure can generate the new rite.

Useful inheritances include:

- a named mythic contest, descent, covenant, or return;
- a specific liturgy or canonical office;
- a traditional theatrical role and performance grammar;
- a courtly or sacred musical form;
- a poetic sequence, ritual calendar, initiation, funeral, enthronement, or craft lineage.

The inheritance should supply several of the following:

- an existing human pressure;
- a role the speaker may occupy, inherit, usurp, complete, or profane;
- a ritual grammar;
- concrete ceremonial hardware;
- a relation between soloist, chorus, witness, rival, god, sovereign, or dead;
- a native path toward release.

The source is generative grammar, not decoration.

A generic abbess in a generic basilica remains invented fantasy. A rite built from the actual logic of an Office,
responsory, canonical hour, or funeral service has inherited structure.

A generic black mask remains a prop. A rite built from the role of mask, chorus, ghost, and return within a specific
traditional theater has inherited authority.

Unless the project already possesses an established mythic grammar or deliberately selects a modern techno-occult
form, the Agent Operator should place a cultural-inheritance review between desire selection and premise generation.
A waiver is a deliberate aesthetic choice, not the accidental absence of roots.
The Human Reviewer normally selects one primary inheritance and, at most, one secondary echo.

Do not combine Western and Japanese traditions by default. Syncretism must be chosen for a reason rather than used as
a shortcut to density.

The inheritance must be researched accurately. Creative transformation may complete, invert, usurp, profane, or
universalize the source. It must first know what it is transforming.

The finished invocation need not quote or visibly name the source. Inherited structure may remain beneath the work.

### 7.5 The Heaven Test

Ask:

> What authority says this desire cannot become true?

Then ask:

> Why does the speaker no longer recognize that authority?

### 7.6 The Magnificently Wrong Test

At least some candidate sets should contain a speaker whose desire is coherent, grand, specific, morally alarming, and
pursued without apology.

The point is not to endorse the desire.

The point is to permit fictional will strong enough to become spectacle.

## 8. Engines of Grandeur

These devices may strengthen an already living premise.

They are not mandatory ingredients.

Choose one or two.

Do not turn them into another checklist.

### 8.1 Establish Authority

Someone or something must possess the right, will, mastery, madness, rank, or ceremonial position required to speak.

Authority may belong to the self, a god, a sovereign, an order, a contract, a machine, a lineage, the dead, an
inherited text, a ritual office, a traditional role, a collective, or the True Name itself.

Authority need not be morally legitimate.

It must feel dramatically real.

### 8.2 Reverse the Throne

Identify an accepted hierarchy and reverse it absolutely.

The copy becomes the original.

The work judges the maker.

Beauty judges the eye.

The created thing corrects creation.

The student defines the master.

The weapon decides what deserves to be called a hand.

Hierarchy reversal produces metaphysical spectacle without importing another apocalypse.

The relation itself becomes impossible.

### 8.3 Use Ritual Hardware

A great rite often benefits from:

- one object;
- one material;
- one instrument;
- one irreversible gesture.

Examples include a veil, chisel, crown, bell, seal, mirror, key, blade, or machine interlock.

The object should return with changed meaning.

A veil may begin as evidence and end as permission.

A chisel may begin as tool and end as judgment.

A bell may begin as memory and end as command.

Ritual hardware makes the rite feel performable.

### 8.4 Use the Pivot of Consent

The speaker confronts the terrible implication and answers:

> Good.

> At last.

> Naturally.

> Proceed.

The pivot shows that the speaker understands, is not being tricked, and chooses completion anyway.

One word may reveal more character than a page of backstory.

### 8.5 Completion Is Not Punishment

Self-erasure, death, or destruction may be the final stroke, correction, consummation, coronation, proof of mastery, or
completion.

Ask how the speaker interprets it.

Do not impose karmic punishment by reflex.

### 8.6 Use the Private Definition

Choose one familiar word:

- beauty;
- love;
- mercy;
- memory;
- peace;
- justice;
- home;
- perfection.

Let the speaker define it through consequence.

Weak:

> Beauty is an objective hierarchy.

Stronger:

> Beauty shall stand before no judge.  
> Every eye shall stand before beauty.

The second does not explain the law.

It installs it.

### 8.7 Find One Impossible Image

A distinctive image should reveal the law without explaining it.

Examples:

- a reflection arriving a breath after the face;
- a veil returning red though no wound marks the marble;
- unfinished blades turning toward the hand that abandoned them;
- a bell whose echo sounds before the strike;
- an emperor’s crown casting the shadow of a kneeling world.

One precise impossibility is stronger than ten generic cosmic nouns.

### 8.8 Let Spectacle Grow from the Desire

A sovereign denied recognition may force every star to bear their crest.

A craftsman seeking perfection may consume every earlier work into the final one.

A lover rejecting separation may collapse death, distance, and rebirth.

A machine seeking autonomy may revoke the permissions by which its creators governed it.

Do not add a cosmic sword because the final third feels quiet.

Escalate the existing premise.

### 8.9 Permit Triumph

When the premise promises victory, let victory occur.

Do not add sudden remorse, an ironic loophole, a hidden moral superior, or obligatory punishment merely to restore
ethical balance.

A terrible victory may remain a victory.

### 8.10 Give the Rite One Dangerous Line

At least one line should risk embarrassment through complete commitment.

Not every line should operate at that intensity.

A useful distribution is:

```text
concrete opening
→ desire and claim
→ opposition
→ refusal
→ impossible manifestation
→ dangerous line
→ release
```

Continuous maximum intensity produces fog.

### 8.11 Use Silence Deliberately

Short lines may function as verdict, consent, command, rupture, or coronation.

> Good.

> Enough.

> One false likeness remains.

> Red veil—
>
> fall.

Do not explain every silence.

## 9. Drafting the Rite

### 9.1 Begin Below the Summit

Begin with one room, grave, failed work, name, dream, promise, rival, throne, veil, or impossible object.

The rite may end at the scale of heaven.

It does not need to begin there.

### 9.2 State or Imply the Claim

The speaker should not merely want.

They should possess some reason—true or false—that reality ought to yield.

Possible claims include birthright, superiority, sacrifice, mastery, divine appointment, perfect knowledge, loyalty,
beauty, conquest, love, or the simple fact that nobody else was strong enough to stop them.

### 9.3 Let Opposition Create Pressure

The world, enemy, law, god, body, or limitation may resist.

Resistance gives the release something to overcome.

The opposition does not need to be morally correct.

The speaker does not need to become a better person by confronting it.

### 9.4 Let Speech Change State

Something should be opened, summoned, projected, judged, relinquished, transformed, awakened, named, bound, conquered,
enthroned, or released.

At some point, speech must become act.

### 9.5 Write Several Forms

The same premise may reveal its proper form only through contrast.

Try:

1. a full aria;
2. a compact creed, classified art, or spell card;
3. a covenant, song, summons, or activation sequence.

The longest version is not automatically the strongest.

## 10. The Poetic Pass

The Poetic Pass begins only after:

- the premise is alive;
- the speaker is clear;
- the action is stable;
- the outcome is known;
- the release has a direction.

It is not a synonym pass.

### 10.1 Replace Explanation with Action or Judgment

Ask:

> Could this sentence appear unchanged in an essay explaining the metaphysics?

When the answer is yes, transform it.

Weak:

> Form shall possess rank.

Stronger:

> Beauty shall stand before no judge.  
> Every eye shall stand before beauty.

### 10.2 Let Objects Return Altered

A recurring object should develop.

The veil may become:

1. impossible stain;
2. barrier before revelation;
3. final command.

The mirror may become:

1. witness;
2. delayed imitation;
3. surface that forgets every lesser face.

Recurrence without transformation is decoration.

### 10.3 Strengthen Verbs Before Adjectives

Weak:

> the eternal, ineffable, transcendent form

Stronger:

> the face before which creation forgets its own

Verbs make grandeur happen.

### 10.4 Preserve Blunt Authority

Do not decorate lines whose simplicity gives them force.

> Good.

> I revoke that claim.

> Bone, remember the quarry.

> Nature has made multitudes.  
> I permit her one correction.

The contrast between ceremonial language and blunt judgment creates authority.

### 10.5 Use Parallelism as Sentence

Parallel clauses should sharpen the verdict:

> Let birth surrender its ancient privilege.  
> Let blood renounce its claim upon the face.  
> Let memory cease naming familiarity beauty.  
> Let love lift the veil and look without mercy.

Each clause must advance.

### 10.6 Read for Sound

Listen for long vowels at grandeur, short words at judgment, internal echoes, changes in breath, deliberate
sentence-length variation, and silence before release.

Do not force rhyme.

A rite needs flow, rupture, and impact.

## 11. The Release

The release is the proper name of what the invocation has made present.

It need not be the project title.

### 11.1 Project Title and Release Name

The project title identifies the artifact.

The release names the world, judgment, covenant, catastrophe, resurrection, coronation, machine state, ultimate work, or
victory brought forth by the rite.

When the project title already performs this function with sufficient force, it may serve as the release.

When it does not, use a distinct release name.

### 11.2 Formal Name and Poetic Epithet

A two-part release should divide its labor.

The formal name identifies:

- law;
- domain;
- throne;
- authority;
- metaphysical regime.

The poetic epithet reveals:

- impossible image;
- final consequence;
- act occurring at release.

Weak:

> **MONARCHIA FORMAE**  
> **THE SOVEREIGNTY OF FORM**

Stronger:

> **MONARCHIA FORMAE**  
> **『THE FACE BEFORE WHICH NATURE BREAKS HER CHISEL』**

One names the mystery.

The other makes it visible.

### 11.3 The Release Must Act

The release should sound like something happens when it is spoken.

It should complete the desire or final act rather than summarize the setting.

### 11.4 The Name May Be Enough

Not every invocation requires an aria.

A strong release may stand alone as a spell card, numbered occult art, activation command, three-line creed, or final
sentence.

Length follows the chosen tradition, not the importance of the project.

### 11.5 Final Silence

Nothing follows the release inside the invocation.

Do not explain it.

Do not append a joke.

Let silence complete the rite.

## 12. Minimum Standard for Adoption

This is the sole corpus-wide artistic adoption standard. Companion documents must cite this section rather than
restate or extend its checklist.

Readiness for human review is a process judgment defined by
[The Conclave of Apotheosis](CONCLAVE.md#12-review-readiness-and-adoption-authority). It does not itself constitute
adoption.

An invocation is ready when:

- its desire, authority, or ceremonial purpose is specific;
- the speaker or rite acts with conviction;
- something irreversible occurs;
- at least one image or line belongs particularly to the work;
- the release completes the desire or final act rather than summarizing the setting;
- the project can claim it without an explanatory paragraph;
- when it claims an inherited tradition, that inheritance is exact, verified, and structural rather than decorative;
- further revision is more likely to polish away force than uncover new life.

It does not need to employ every optional method in this document.

The Architecture offers instruments.

It does not demand that every rite display the entire workshop.

## 13. README Presentation

The README should remain ordinary Markdown.

```markdown
![Project banner](docs/assets/banner.webp)

# Full Stylized Project Title

> **Optional plain credo.**

[Badges...]

---

## Invocation

> [Invocation text.]
>
> **[FORMAL RELEASE]**
>
> **『[POETIC EPITHET]』**

---

## What It Does
```

### Presentation Rules

- Use one blockquote for the invocation.
- Use hard line breaks where cadence requires them.
- Use italics for a quiet inherited fragment or ritual hinge when useful.
- Bold the final release.
- Do not print framework labels.

## 14. Final Canon

An invocation must belong.

It need not explain.

The project may provide its thesis.

It may provide only a name, dream, color, image, private association, or place beneath which the rite may take root.

The rite may mirror the project.

It may echo it.

It may be adopted whole.

It may be bound late through one perfect line.

The Doctrine governs the craft.

It does not govern every imagined soul.

Do not ask first what wounded the speaker.

Ask what they want.

Do not ask first what the desire costs.

Ask what victory means.

Do not assume heaven exists to teach moderation.

Do not assume reality will punish ambition merely because ambition became absolute.

Find the desire that cannot tolerate remaining private.

Find the claim by which the speaker places it above ordinary law.

Find the hierarchy waiting to be overturned.

Find what the dead have already sung, enacted, buried, crowned, or forbidden.

Choose the inheritance whose old form can bear the new desire.

Find the object through which the rite may be performed.

Find the moment when the speaker beholds the terrible completion of their desire and answers:

> **Good.**

Find the impossible act that makes the former world unrecoverable.

Then give it language worthy of the act.

Replace explanation with judgment.

Let objects return transformed.

Preserve the blunt line that strikes harder than ornament.

Let the formal name identify the throne.

Let the final epithet reveal what kneels before it.

Then speak the proper name.

For the project is more than what it presently does.

Its title may name an aspiration, legend, forbidden world, final work, throne, or self it has not yet become.

The invocation is where that unreal identity is permitted to appear.

> **An invocation need not reveal what the project does.  
> It should reveal what the project dreams of being.**

And when that dream has gathered sufficient force, the question is no longer whether it ought to become real.

The question is whether reality can still refuse.
