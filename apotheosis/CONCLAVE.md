# The Conclave of Apotheosis

*An orchestration protocol for human-reviewed agent creation, fully agent creation, and the preservation of dangerous
answers*

> **Agents create the possibilities.  
> The human decides which possibility earns another form.**

> **Generate broadly at low resolution.  
> Spend prose only after selection.**

This document implements [The Architecture of Apotheosis](README.md).

A compact ChatGPT.com path is available in [A Short Walkthrough for ChatGPT.com](WALKTHROUGH.md).

The Conclave is normative for orchestration and production process. The Architecture remains normative for artistic
adoption, including the sole [Minimum Standard for Adoption](README.md#12-minimum-standard-for-adoption).

The corpus recognizes exactly three operating forms, in descending order of priority:

1. **Human-reviewed, agent-written, agent-orchestrated** — agents generate, research, select internally, develop,
   criticize, and revise; a human reviews the principal creative narrowing points and alone grants final adoption.
2. **Fully agent** — agents perform orchestration, independent selection, criticism, revision, and adoption without
   human gates.
3. **Fully human** — one human performs the same artistic stages directly.

In the primary form, the human is neither the writer nor the Operator.

The human is the aesthetic authority at the principal selection boundaries.

The central operational law is:

> **Generation, selection, criticism, and revision must not collapse into one undifferentiated act performed in one
> context.**

Reject this process:

```text
one agent
→ generates a few elaborate ideas
→ silently chooses the safest one
→ writes a long invocation
→ explains why it followed the guide
→ critiques itself
→ reports success
```

Prefer this process:

```text
Human Commission
→ broad internal desire generation
→ 12–16 short Desire Cards
→ Human Desire Review
→ canon-first research and confrontation generation
→ 10 short Canonical Confrontation Cards
→ Human Canonical Review
→ 8 short Premise Thumbnails
→ Human Premise Review
→ 6 Ritual Voice Sketches
→ Human Sketch Review
→ 2 Full Invocations
→ Human Spine Review
→ Structural Critic, Canonical Accuracy Reviewer, Binder when needed, one-spine Integrator
→ Register Critic, Baroque Editor when warranted, Poetic Editor, Integrity Reviewer
→ 1 Final Candidate
→ Human Adoption Review
```

The same model may fill more than one role only through isolated contexts.

A fresh conversation with the same model may count as a new role.

Continuing in one contaminated thread does not.

On ChatGPT.com, fresh isolation normally means Temporary Chats outside the Project. The Operator prepares complete
copy-paste prompts; the Human Reviewer acts only as a mechanical courier and returns the outputs to the Operator
chat. The Operator must not claim to have run isolated specialists when it merely simulated them in one context.

## 1. Operating Forms

### 1.1 Human-Reviewed, Agent-Written, Agent-Orchestrated

This is the default.

The human supplies a sparse natural-language Commission and reviews six irreversible creative selections:

1. **Animating desire selection**
2. **Canonical confrontation selection**
3. **Premise selection**
4. **Ritual voice sketch selection**
5. **Full invocation or spine selection**
6. **Final adoption**

The Agent Operator performs everything else:

- derives project context and explicit human constraints;
- constructs every internal packet;
- generates large, varied internal pools;
- compresses them into short human-facing cards;
- derives and locks the Commission Invariant after desire selection unless the human supplied a fixed outcome;
- researches exact pre-1900 Western and Japanese sources;
- enforces the canon-first source policy;
- isolates specialist contexts;
- on ChatGPT.com, prepares complete copy-paste prompts for fresh Temporary Chats and waits for the human to return
  their outputs rather than simulating isolation inside the Operator chat;
- uses independent Selectors at every narrowing point;
- rejects invariant-breaking candidates before artistic ranking;
- develops only selected candidates;
- conducts structural criticism, source accuracy review, binding, one-spine integration, register criticism,
  source-responsive editing, poetic revision, and integrity review;
- interprets brief human reactions;
- returns the work for final adoption.

The human is not expected to invent desires, formulate premises, research sources, write the invocation, read dossiers,
manage agents, prepare packets, author rejection conditions, or diagnose which specialist role should run next.

When ChatGPT.com requires manual chat creation, the human may open Temporary Chats, paste the Operator's complete
prompts, and return the outputs. This courier step is mechanical; the Human Reviewer does not design prompts, choose
roles, or curate the raw specialist field.

The human may respond with as little as:

> 2, 7, and 11. Mostly 7.

> Source 4, but steal the prohibition from 9.

> Premise C. Keep the rival alive.

> Sketch 5 has the voice. Keep the release seed from 2.

> Draft B is the spine. The English epithet is weak.

> Adopt.

The human may give more direction when desired. Doing so does not create a fourth operating form.

### 1.2 Fully Agent

The same staged architecture remains, but independent agent Selectors replace the Human Reviewer at every principal
selection boundary.

A fully agent conclave must still preserve:

- isolated roles;
- visible internal candidate sets;
- progressive resolution;
- source distribution and pre-1900 constraints;
- selection boundaries external to the generator;
- high-variance candidates;
- the locked Commission Invariant in every later packet;
- binary rejection before artistic ranking;
- one-spine integration;
- independent criticism;
- separate Register Critic, Baroque Editor, Poetic Editor, and Integrity Reviewer contexts;
- explicit adoption under the Architecture's standard.

The absence of human review does not permit one agent to generate, select, criticize, and adopt its own answer in one
context.

### 1.3 Fully Human

A human may perform the same stages personally:

```text
commission
→ broad desires
→ selected desire
→ canonical confrontations
→ selected source
→ premise thumbnails
→ selected premise
→ ritual voice sketches
→ selected ritual form
→ full drafts
→ selected spine
→ structural and register criticism
→ source-responsive revision and integrity review
→ adoption
```

This form is supported in Appendix A. It is not the primary use case.

### 1.4 No Hidden Fourth Mode

A Human Reviewer may occasionally provide detailed direction.

A fully agent run may later be reopened by a human.

A human author may use agents for criticism.

These are interactions inside the three forms, not additional operating forms.

## 2. Progressive-Resolution Quickstart

The Conclave should expose many possibilities while they are cheap to inspect.

| Gate | Human sees | Default count | Maximum visible size |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Desire | Desire Cards | 12–16 | 25–40 words each |
| Canon | Canonical Confrontation Cards | 10 | 40–60 words each |
| Premise | Premise Thumbnails | 8 | 60–90 words each |
| Voice sketch | Ritual Voice Sketches | 6 | 70–120 words each |
| Full draft | Finished Invocations | 2 | 160–320 words each |
| Final | Revised Candidate | 1 | As required |

The Operator may generate much larger pools internally.

Four isolated Desire Generators may produce thirty-two raw desires. Several Canonical Scouts may produce dozens of
sources. The Human Reviewer sees only the compact, deduplicated field.

The Operator may curate for clarity and range.

It must not silently choose the creative direction before the relevant human gate.

### 2.1 Desire Cards

A Desire Card contains only:

- a short title;
- what the speaker wants;
- one sentence describing complete victory.

It contains no character name, backstory, price, cultural source, risk analysis, or miniature setting.

Example form:

```markdown
1. **The Sole Original**

   To create one form so perfect that nature loses the right to call itself
   original.

   **Victory:** every living likeness is recognized as an imperfect copy.
```

The visible field should normally contain:

- three sovereign, imperial, prideful, or blasphemous desires;
- three vocation, beauty, discovery, or rivalry desires;
- two love, loyalty, devotion, or possession desires;
- two inhuman, institutional, divine, or collective desires;
- two to six wild cards.

### 2.2 Canonical Confrontation Cards

The default source policy is **canon-first, literature-first, pre-1900, and Western-weighted**.

Unless the Commission points elsewhere, present ten cards:

- **six Western literary sources**;
- **three Japanese literary sources**;
- **one Western or Japanese liturgical, operatic, theatrical, or ritual wild card**.

Each card contains only:

- the exact named source and charged scene;
- the authority or proposition it already possesses;
- the dramatic locus inside the source: figure, law, prohibition, object, voice, place, or event;
- what the new speaker completes, contradicts, profanes, surpasses, or seizes there;
- one charged image or object.

Example form:

```markdown
1. **Orpheus — The Backward Glance Becomes Command**

   Death once permitted song to lead one soul upward, provided the singer
   never turned. The new singer turns deliberately: the forbidden glance
   becomes the signal by which every dead voice must follow.

   **Locus:** the prohibition against looking back.  
   **Image:** footsteps continuing after the singer has stopped.
```

The card is not a bibliography or dossier.

The source may remain visibly named in the finished rite. The speaker should normally inhabit or usurp the
canonical world rather than address its historical author from outside it.

### 2.3 Premise Thumbnails

A Premise Thumbnail contains:

- the speaker or speaking authority;
- the claim upon reality;
- the complete victory;
- the irreversible final act;
- one recurring image.

It contains no `Protect`, `Risk`, process analysis, source bibliography, or explanation of project symbolism.

### 2.4 Ritual Voice Sketches

A Ritual Voice Sketch tests rhetorical register before the Operator spends a full aria on it.

Each sketch contains:

- a possible opening cadence;
- one movement of canonical confrontation;
- one movement of self-mythologizing;
- a legible speaker office or identity;
- one command answered visibly or one concrete stage transformation;
- one specimen of spectacle;
- a release seed.

A sketch should normally be 70–120 words. It may omit connective material.

The six sketches should test genuinely different source-responsive voices rather than six shortened versions of the
same modern monologue.

The six sketches should vary how the speaker enters the canonical world:

- address a canonical figure;
- continue the charged scene from within;
- violate or reverse a prohibition;
- seize an object, voice, role, or office;
- use the source's rhetorical form without direct address;
- attempt one source-responsive wild card.

At most one sketch may address the historical author, and only when authorship itself is central to the premise and the
Charged Source Packet explicitly permits it.

For an ornate Western source, the rhetorical directions may include baroque apostrophe, prophetic liturgy, operatic
recitative moving into aria, disputation or counter-wager, Romantic visionary excess, and a source-responsive wild
card.

For an austere or compressed source, the Operator should choose variants native to that source instead.

The Human Reviewer selects the rhetorical voice, dramatic locus, source treatment, and release seed before full
drafting.

### 2.5 Full Invocations

Only after sketch selection should the Operator commission full invocations.

Produce exactly two by default:

1. one faithful development of the selected sketch;
2. one deliberately different structural alternative using the same approved premise and canonical source.

The human chooses the spine and may protect exact lines.

### 2.6 Final Candidate

After spine selection, the Operator normally runs:

1. a hostile Structural Critic and Canonical Accuracy Reviewer;
2. a Binder when project affinity is weak;
3. a one-spine Integrator that performs only authorized structural changes;
4. a Register Critic;
5. a Baroque Editor or another source-responsive Register Editor;
6. a Poetic Editor;
7. an Integrity Reviewer that returns the minimally corrected final text.

Finishing occurs after structural integration so later synthesis cannot smooth away the approved register.

The Human Reviewer receives one final candidate for adoption.

### 2.7 Human Input Should Remain Small

At every gate, the human may:

- select by number or letter;
- select several and name a favorite;
- protect one line or image;
- reject the field;
- give one brief reaction.

The Operator translates that reaction into process.

### 2.8 Maximum Revision Rule

After two substantial revision cycles, stop and ask whether the premise remains alive.

When revision produces only cleaner language, larger spectacle, or better compliance, abandon the premise.

## 3. The Human Reviewer

The Human Reviewer supplies taste and final authority.

### 3.1 Responsibilities

The Reviewer:

- gives the sparse Commission;
- selects at the six principal gates;
- names lines or images that must survive;
- approves or rejects final adoption.

The Reviewer is not required to:

- prepare packets;
- research sources;
- manage agents;
- explain every reaction;
- write the invocation.

### 3.2 Productive Reactions

Useful reactions include:

- “This one has life.”
- “Too tasteful.”
- “The speaker became a victim again.”
- “Keep the exact accusation.”
- “The source is furniture, not an opponent.”
- “More spectacle, but derive it from the lyre.”
- “The premise works; the English is flat.”
- “This is still polished modern English. Give it the source's full rhetorical temperature.”
- “More baroque, but ornament the speaker's law rather than the cosmos.”
- “The clipped verdicts feel like house style.”
- “Draft B is the spine.”
- “The formal name works; the epithet does not.”
- “Reject the field.”

The human does not need to turn these judgments into universal theory.

### 3.3 Harmful Overcorrections

The Reviewer should avoid:

- forwarding the entire failure history to a creative writer;
- converting each reaction into ten mandatory rules;
- asking the writer to satisfy every criticism simultaneously;
- reopening the premise during a local register or baroque pass;
- selecting only by obvious project relevance.

## 4. The Agent Operator

The Agent Operator orchestrates the first two operating forms.

### 4.1 Commission Interpretation

A sparse Commission may be as small as:

> Create a README invocation for this project. Make it grand, theatrical,
> canonically charged, and unapologetically chuuni. Avoid direct software
> allegory and polished word salad. When the selected source is ornate, do
> not flatten it into smooth contemporary dramatic prose.

The Operator derives internally:

- project identity;
- explicit hard constraints;
- taboos;
- candidate-generation batches;
- the eventual Commission Invariant;
- specialist prompts and packets.

It must not answer a broad Commission with a questionnaire when ambiguity can be resolved through candidate fields.

### 4.2 Canon-First Source Policy

The normal source order is:

1. a specific pre-1900 literary work, scene, poem, mythic episode, or dramatic text;
2. a specific liturgy, opera, Noh play, ritual text, or court performance;
3. a broad tradition only as secondary support;
4. generic cultural atmosphere only by explicit waiver.

Prefer:

- *Atsumori*, not “Noh masks and ghosts”;
- Orpheus's forbidden glance, not “Greek mystery atmosphere”;
- Faust's wager, not “German occultism”;
- Lear on the heath, not “tragic kingship”;
- the Office of the Dead, not “a basilica”;
- *The Tale of the Heike*, not “samurai elegy.”

The default source field is Western-weighted and literature-first: six Western literary or sacred-canonical cards,
three classical Japanese literary cards, and one ritual or theatrical wild card.

The source should supply both:

- **scene:** the charged event, prohibition, claim, or confrontation;
- **form:** the rhetorical or ritual architecture by which the new rite speaks.

Possible forms include:

- Greek choral ode;
- tragic accusation and response;
- Psalmic parallelism;
- liturgical antiphon;
- prophetic denunciation;
- medieval vision;
- Shakespearean soliloquy;
- Miltonic invocation;
- Faustian wager;
- Romantic apostrophe;
- operatic recitative and aria;
- waka compression and pivot;
- monogatari lament;
- Noh gradual revelation;
- norito cadence;
- warrior-tale death song.

The Canonical Scout should normally search within a broad internal territory such as:

**Western:** Homeric epic, Greek tragedy, Ovid, Virgil, Hebrew and Christian scripture, Psalms, Offices and Requiems,
Dante, Arthurian romance, medieval vision literature, Shakespeare, Milton, metaphysical poetry, Goethe, German
Romanticism, nineteenth-century opera, and major Romantic or philosophical-poetic works.

**Japanese:** *Kojiki*, *Nihon Shoki*, *Man’yōshū*, *Kokinshū*, *The Tale of Genji*, *The Tale of the Heike*, exact
Noh plays, medieval Buddhist tales, *Chūshingura*, Bashō, Saikaku, Ueda Akinari, and other pre-1900 court, warrior,
ghost, and poetic texts.

This search territory belongs to the Scout. It should not be forwarded as a menu to the creative writer.

One primary cultural field should govern the rite.

Cross-cultural combinations require a deliberate reason. Do not create an international buffet of Greek myth, Noh
structure, Latin release, shrine imagery, and cathedral furniture.

The source may remain conspicuously visible.

The test is not whether it has been hidden.

The test is whether the new speaker has done something audacious enough to stand beside it.

The source does not normally mean the historical author. The speaker should behave as an inhabitant, claimant, rival,
heretic, or usurper inside the canonical world. Address a character, law, prohibition, object, voice, place, role, or
event by default. Direct author address is permitted only when authorship, incompletion, dictation, textual ownership,
or the conflict between author and work is essential to the manifested law.

### 4.3 Responsibilities

The Operator:

- generates broad internal pools;
- presents short, varied review surfaces;
- keeps research backstage;
- protects the Commission Invariant;
- enforces source accuracy without demanding demonstrative completeness;
- defines a dramatic locus inside the source and keeps historical-author address exceptional;
- preserves high-variance candidates;
- develops only selected work;
- uses one-spine integration;
- protects the selected Register Packet through drafting and revision;
- ensures that a canonical argument becomes an onstage event and that the speaker possesses an office inside the
  manifested law;
- prepares and routes fresh Temporary Chat prompts when operating on ChatGPT.com;
- routes human reactions to the correct stage;
- stops when revision is polishing a dead premise.

### 4.4 Prohibitions

The Operator must not:

- expose internal packets as human forms;
- treat “inheritance” as the default plot of source use;
- present generic high-culture furniture as a canonical anchor;
- treat the historical author as the default canonical opponent or addressee;
- let the source's native moral replace the required victory;
- send full research dossiers to creative writers;
- elaborate unselected candidates;
- let one agent generate and silently select its own safest answer;
- ask the Human Reviewer to invent specialist prompts or packet fields;
- average several drafts into one smooth median;
- use a generic poetry pass to smooth source-responsive difficulty into modern prose;
- require citations or rationale in the finished invocation;
- explain the project connection after the rite;

### 4.5 Additional Escalation

Outside the six mandatory gates, the Operator interrupts only when:

- the Commission contains mutually incompatible hard constraints;
- no candidate passes the invariant;
- source research reveals a factual or ethical problem that changes the premise;
- two final directions are equally strong and fundamentally incompatible;
- two major revision cycles fail.

### 4.6 ChatGPT.com Temporary-Chat Boundary

In the primary ChatGPT.com form, the Agent Operator cannot open or populate additional chats on the human's behalf.
It must orchestrate divergent specialist work through complete copy-paste prompts for fresh Temporary Chats.

At each divergent stage, the Operator must:

1. prepare two to four self-contained prompts for the next specialist role;
2. stop and ask the Human Reviewer to run each prompt in a fresh Temporary Chat;
3. receive the raw outputs without human editing, summarization, or ranking;
4. preserve, deduplicate, curate, and route those outputs itself.

Use fresh Temporary Chats by default for:

- Desire Generators;
- Canonical Scouts;
- Premise Builders;
- Ritual Sketchers;
- full Ritual Writers;
- Structural Critics;
- Canonical Accuracy Reviewers;
- Register Critics;
- Baroque Editors;
- Poetic Editors;
- Integrity Reviewers.

The Operator chat may handle deterministic curation, packet construction, human review surfaces, feedback routing,
one-spine integration, and clerical changes.

A specialist Temporary Chat does not automatically receive the Project's files or prior control-room context. Every
copy-paste prompt must therefore contain the complete packet and constraints required by that role. The Human Reviewer
performs only the mechanical transfer. If enabled global custom instructions would distort a specialist, disable them
for that Temporary Chat.

Single-chat execution is an explicit fallback, not the default. It is permitted only when:

- the Human Reviewer explicitly requests it;
- the platform exposes genuine isolated subagents or contexts instead;
- the operation is clerical, deterministic, or integrative rather than divergent.

When single-chat execution is used, the Operator must label the run **reduced isolation** and must not describe
sequential role simulation as independent specialist work.

## 5. Specialist Roles

Each role receives only the context required to perform it.

### 5.1 Desire Generator

**Purpose:** Produce raw desires and complete victories.

**Receives:** mood, project identity if useful, batch type, taboo list.

**Produces:** short desires only.

**Must not produce:** names, backstories, costs, source assignments, premises, or invocations.

### 5.2 Canonical Scout

**Purpose:** Find exact pre-1900 sources capable of resisting or carrying the selected desire.

**Receives:** selected desire, Commission Invariant, source distribution, research standards.

**Produces:** backstage source dossiers with citations, factual notes, candidate dramatic loci, and an explicit
assessment of whether historical-author address is premise-relevant.

**Must not:** invent fantasy cultures, offer generic atmosphere, or write premises.

### 5.3 Canonical Confrontation Distiller

**Purpose:** Convert source research into short human-facing cards.

**Produces:** ten cards containing exact source, internal dramatic locus, source claim, act of confrontation, and
charged image. Backstage, it also identifies any governing word or proposition whose grammar carries the source's
authority.

**Must not:** expose bibliography, glossary, or research apparatus, or turn every source into a rebuke of its
historical author.

### 5.4 Premise Builder

**Purpose:** Build short fictional foundations from the selected desire and canonical source.

**Receives:** Commission Invariant and Charged Source Packet.

**Produces:** Premise Thumbnails only. Each internal premise must identify the speaker's metaphysical office, the
argument-to-event transition, and the visible proof of victory.

**Must not:** write invocations, explain software symbolism, or leave the speaker as merely the cleverest reader of
the source.

### 5.5 Register Designer

**Purpose:** Convert the selected source, premise, and human register request into a protected Register Packet.

**Produces:** rhetorical temperature, dramatic locus, permitted addressees, author-address authorization, syntax,
ceremonial devices, speaker posture, metaphysical office, ornament focus, argument-to-event trajectory, forbidden
modern tics, forbidden generic maximalism, and protected irregularities.

**Must not:** write the invocation or copy recognizable wording from the source material.

### 5.6 Ritual Sketcher

**Purpose:** Test several rhetorical voices and release architectures at low resolution.

**Receives:** selected premise, Charged Source Packet, and Register Packet.

**Produces:** six Ritual Voice Sketches that vary both rhetorical voice and the speaker's dramatic relationship to
the source. Every sketch must show how the canonical argument becomes an event and how the speaker's office appears
within the rite.

**Must not:** fill every gap, write long lore, explain the source, default to six polished contemporary monologues, or
address the historical author unless the packet explicitly authorizes it.

### 5.7 Ritual Writer

**Purpose:** Expand the selected voice sketch into two finished invocations.

**Receives:** selected premise, selected sketch, protected lines, Charged Source Packet, and Register Packet.

**Produces:** two full drafts with distinct structures, the protected rhetorical temperature, a legible speaker
identity or office, a visible answered command, and a final tableau proving that the victory has already occurred.

**Must preserve:** the selected dramatic locus. The drafts must not transfer the confrontation to the historical
author unless the Human Reviewer explicitly selected authorship as the conflict. A one-part release is permitted and
should not be expanded into a weaker diptych merely for consistency.

**Must enact:** at least one commanded transformation before the release. The final tableau must prove the claim
physically or theatrically; an ornate argument alone is insufficient.

### 5.8 Selector

**Purpose:** Curate without rewriting.

**Produces:** passing candidates, exact reasons, and preserved high-variance alternatives.

**Must not:** choose only for polish, sympathy, obvious project relevance, or contemporary readability.

### 5.9 Binder

**Purpose:** Add minimal project affinity to a living rite.

**Produces:** up to three changes, each affecting no more than two lines or one release component.

### 5.10 Structural Critic

**Purpose:** Diagnose premise, action, spectacle, source use, and release before revision.

**Receives:** full guide, actual draft, premise, source packet, preserve list.

**Produces:** the living line, local structural failures, cuts, smallest decisive correction, and verdicts on whether
the speaker inhabits the source, possesses a metaphysical office, and turns the governing claim into witnessed
action rather than an ornate brief.

**Must ask:** if the historical author is addressed, is authorship itself indispensable to the manifested law?

### 5.11 Canonical Accuracy Reviewer

**Purpose:** Verify source use before the Register and Baroque passes magnify it.

**Must verify:** exact proper nouns, places, relationships, quotations, ritual offices, and source claims. One damaged
name or invented fact can break the authority of an otherwise powerful opening.

**May:** correct or remove inaccurate material.

**Must not:** demand extra terminology, historical exposition, author address, or unused ceremonial details merely
to display research.

### 5.12 Register Critic

**Purpose:** Diagnose whether the English has flattened into polished contemporary dramatic prose.

**Produces:** modern smoothing, stock mannerisms, balanced slogan chains, legal or academic abstraction, lost
rhetorical temperature, places for authorized apostrophe or self-coronation, meta-literary commentary, unjustified
author address, empty density, protected irregularities, and the passages that most need a Baroque Pass.

**Must not:** rewrite or demand restraint merely for literary respectability.

### 5.13 Baroque Editor

**Purpose:** Increase source-responsive density, ornament, apostrophe, self-mythology, theatrical force, and the
transformation of semantic argument into ceremonial action.

**Must preserve:** premise, action, victory, source confrontation, symbolic world, and release direction.

**Must intensify when needed:** the speaker's self-created office, the visible fulfillment of commands, and the final
tableau proving that the old canonical grammar has failed.

**Must not:** add stock cosmic submission, unrelated lore, a new symbolic system, or direct address to the historical
author unless the selected packet explicitly authorizes it.

### 5.14 Poetic Editor

**Purpose:** Improve exact image, verbs, sound, recurring objects, and release language after the register is correct.

**Must preserve:** approved density, jaggedness, asymmetry, speaker posture, source-responsive rhetoric, stage action,
and the selected single-part or two-part release architecture.

**Must not:** smooth the invocation into tasteful contemporary prose, turn it into a chain of polished slogans, add a
new premise, source, or symbolic system, or fabricate a second release component merely for consistency.

### 5.15 Integrity Reviewer

**Purpose:** Remove generic maximalist filler without smoothing the work back into contemporary prose. It also rejects
cosmic legal briefs, speakers with claims but no office, imperatives that never receive an answer, and final tableaux
that remain only promised.

**Produces:** a minimally corrected final text or a short list of exact local cuts, according to the Operator's packet.

**Must preserve:** dangerous abundance, irregular syntax, source-responsive ornament, and the speaker's particular
voice.

**Must remove or flag:** the dead author used as a stock opponent when the dramatic locus belongs inside the work.

**Must also flag:** an aria that remains a legal, scholarly, or philosophical argument; balanced slogan chains;
portable formulas such as “I do not receive the old act; I correct it”; commands that never occur; a speaker with no
office beyond being correct; and a two-part release forced by house habit.

### 5.16 Integrator

**Purpose:** Produce the authorized structural synthesis before register finishing.

**Must obey:**

> **Choose one draft as the body.  
> Transplant only named organs.  
> Do not blend the bodies together.**

### 5.17 Implementer

**Purpose:** Insert the approved invocation into the repository.

No generic Implementer prompt is provided. The Implementer follows the target repository's own instructions and receives
only the approved invocation and integration task.

## 6. Selection Boundaries

A valid selection boundary requires:

1. the full internal candidate field remains preserved;
2. another role evaluates it;
3. the human sees the compact review surface in the primary form;
4. selected candidates are named explicitly;
5. no rewrite occurs during selection;
6. unselected candidates are not elaborated unless preserved as reserve.

### 6.1 Desire Boundary

Present 12–16 cards, each no longer than 40 words.

Reject vague desires such as “surpass fate” or “seek truth.”

Preserve sharp, different victories.

### 6.2 Canonical Boundary

Present ten cards, each no longer than 60 words.

Default distribution:

- six Western literary;
- three Japanese literary;
- one liturgical, operatic, theatrical, or ritual wild card.

All sources should be pre-1900 unless the Commission explicitly waives the limit.

Each card should identify an internal dramatic locus. Historical-author address is not a default confrontation mode.

### 6.3 Premise Boundary

Present eight thumbnails, each no longer than 90 words.

Each must preserve the invariant and contain a concrete irreversible act.

### 6.4 Voice-Sketch Boundary

Present six Ritual Voice Sketches, each 70–120 words.

Each must test a genuinely different rhetorical register, dramatic relationship to the source, spectacle movement,
and release seed. No more than one may address the historical author, and only when the packet authorizes it.

### 6.5 Full-Draft Boundary

Present exactly two full invocations by default.

The human selects the spine, protected lines, and any register feature that must survive revision.

### 6.6 Adoption Boundary

Present one revised candidate.

Only the Human Reviewer may grant adoption in the primary form.

### 6.7 Plural Promotion

The Human Reviewer may promote any number of candidates at any gate.

Promotion does not authorize combination. Each promoted candidate remains an independent branch unless the Reviewer
explicitly names an element to transfer.

The Operator must keep the next review surface within its prescribed total budget. It may generate broadly backstage,
but it must not multiply a complete field for every promoted branch.

Candidates may be marked:

## 7. Primary Form: Human-Reviewed Agent Conclave

### 7.1 Sparse Commission

The human names the project, requested artifact, desired register, and any known taboo.

The Operator begins with desire generation, not a questionnaire.

On ChatGPT.com, the Operator announces the Temporary Chat courier workflow, prepares complete specialist prompts,
and stops whenever fresh outputs are required. The human opens the chats and returns the outputs; the Operator still
owns every creative and orchestration decision.

### 7.2 Desire Review

The Operator prepares two to four complete Desire Generator prompts for fresh contexts, receives the raw outputs,
deduplicates them, and presents 12–16 Desire Cards. In an environment with genuine subagents, it may launch those
contexts directly.

The human may select any number, mark favorites, and place others in reserve.

The Operator develops the selected branches within the next gate's total budget and keeps them separate unless the
human explicitly requests a transfer.

The Operator then derives and locks the Commission Invariant.

### 7.3 Canonical Review

The Operator commissions Canonical Scouts through fresh contexts or prepares complete Temporary Chat prompts for
the Human Reviewer to relay.

The Scouts research exact pre-1900 sources with the default Western-weighted distribution.

The Operator presents ten short confrontations.

The human may promote several source confrontations. The Operator asks only which should be primary when the next
creative stage requires one governing source. Others may remain reserve or contribute one explicitly named echo.

### 7.4 Premise Review

The Operator creates a Charged Source Packet and commissions Premise Builders in fresh contexts. On ChatGPT.com, it
prepares the prompts and waits for the Human Reviewer to return the outputs.

The human receives eight Premise Thumbnails and may promote any number as active or reserve branches. The Operator
keeps the next visible field within its total budget and does not blend branches without explicit instruction.

### 7.5 Ritual Voice Review

The Operator constructs a protected Register Packet and commissions Ritual Sketchers in fresh contexts. On
ChatGPT.com, it supplies complete copy-paste prompts rather than simulating the sketches inside the Operator chat.

The human receives six Ritual Voice Sketches and may promote any number. The selected field identifies rhetorical
temperature, source treatment, spectacle, breach, and release seeds without forcing premature synthesis.

### 7.6 Full-Draft Review

The Operator commissions exactly two full invocations in fresh writer contexts. On ChatGPT.com, it prepares the two
writer prompts and asks the Human Reviewer only to relay the outputs.

The human normally selects one spine and may protect exact lines. When both structures remain genuinely alive and
incompatible, both may remain active until the human chooses or explicitly authorizes a transfer.

### 7.7 Autonomous Refinement

The Operator runs structural criticism, source accuracy review, optional binding, and one-spine integration first.
It then routes the spine through fresh Register Critic, Baroque Editor when warranted, Poetic Editor, and Integrity
Reviewer contexts. On ChatGPT.com, the Human Reviewer may relay those prompts mechanically while the Operator owns
all packet construction and editorial decisions.

### 7.8 Adoption Review

The human receives one final candidate and adopts, rejects, or gives one brief reaction.

## 8. Fully Agent Conclave

The fully agent form uses the same progressive-resolution stages.

Independent Selectors replace the Human Reviewer.

A single conversation that merely announces new role names does not satisfy the fully agent form. The environment
must provide genuine isolated contexts or subagents. On ordinary ChatGPT.com, use the primary human-reviewed form
with Temporary Chats unless another tool can actually launch independent agents.

At each stage, the Selector preserves at least:

1. Best Overall
2. Most Dangerous
3. Most Original
4. Strongest Canonical Charge
5. Highest Spectacle
6. Wild Card

The Agent Operator must preserve the actual text of selected and minority candidates.

It may develop more than one branch when selection remains uncertain.

Fully agent adoption still requires the Architecture's standard.

## 9. Context Isolation

### 9.1 Clean Creative Context

For each creative role:

- begin a fresh context;
- provide only the role packet;
- omit failed drafts;
- reduce prior criticism to a short taboo list;
- prohibit framework labels and rationale;
- keep the full guide away from the writer.

### 9.2 Research Stays Backstage

The Canonical Scout may receive the research question and use web search.

The Premise Builder and Ritual Writer should not receive:

- bibliographies;
- full source dossiers;
- instrument inventories;
- ceremonial orders;
- long plot summaries;
- rejected sources;
- the complete source pool.

They receive the Charged Source Packet.

### 9.3 Canonical Skin May Remain Visible

The creative writer may retain:

- the named figure or work;
- the charged scene;
- one recognizable prohibition or object;
- one brief verified public-domain fragment;
- the source's rhetorical form.

Visibility is not dossier leakage.

The source becomes a problem only when the rite demonstrates research instead of committing an act.

### 9.4 Avoid Prompt Contaminants

Do not repeatedly expose creative agents to:

- `inheritance`, `lineage`, `succession`, or `ancestor` unless native to the premise;
- successful signature lines such as *Good.* as reusable instructions;
- the entire failure archive;
- one project as the example in every packet;
- long lists of canonical names.

The Operator may use the list internally. The writer receives one selected source.

---

### 9.5 The Register Travels; the Style Thread Does Not

A stable Register Packet may be copied into fresh specialist contexts.

A long conversation dedicated to “learning the style” should not become the default. It accumulates local mannerisms,
stock verdicts, repeated imagery, and the rhythms of the previous successful draft.

Carry forward:

- the Register Packet;
- selected source and premise;
- protected lines;
- a short taboo list.

Do not carry forward:

- every prior draft;
- the complete style debate;
- signature lines from unrelated manifestations;
- the whole Archaeology;
- a running archive of failed rewrites.

The register should remain stable while the context remains clean.

```text
Do not select solely for polish, sympathy, moral nuance, contemporary
readability, or obvious project relevance. Preserve any number the Human
Reviewer promotes as independent active or reserve branches.
```

### 11.9 Ritual Writer

```text
Write exactly two full invocations from the approved premise and voice sketch.

Draft A should faithfully develop the selected sketch. Draft B should use a
substantially different structure while preserving the same desire, victory,
canonical source, final act, and protected register.

Target 160–320 words each unless the selected form is deliberately compact.

REGISTER LOCK

If the Register Packet names modern fictional register references, use them as
calibration for density, cadence, self-mythologizing posture, and ceremonial
weight only. Do not reproduce their plots, symbols, proper nouns, signature
syntax, or recognizable wording.

Treat this as an actual spoken magical aria, not narrative prose, a modern
dramatic monologue, or elevated project copy.

Required when the Register Packet calls for ornate speech:

- dense, winding, periodic syntax;
- ceremonial repetition and cumulative clauses;
- direct apostrophe to an authorized canonical figure, law, prohibition,
  object, voice, place, event, or cosmic authority;
- extravagant self-coronation;
- a speaker who savors and performs their own metaphysics;
- language closer to heretical scripture, prophetic denunciation, grand opera,
  or metaphysical aria than contemporary literary dialogue;
- willingness to sacrifice easy readability for weight and intoxication;
- ornament derived from the speaker's exact private law.

Forbidden:

- isolated stock interjections such as “No.”, “Good.”, “Enough.”, “Proceed.”,
  or “Begin.”;
- clean contemporary sentence rhythm throughout;
- tasteful minimalist final-boss dialogue;
- smooth cinematic phrasing;
- generic jealous stars, screaming suns, kneeling heavens, universal begging,
  or repeated infinity claims;
- close imitation of recognizable wording from existing fictional chants.

The speaker should behave as though the canonical events are real. Do not turn
the aria into literary criticism or address the historical author unless the
Register Packet explicitly authorizes it.

Do not merely make the cosmos louder. Ornament the speaker's private law.

A semantic or philosophical attack may begin the aria, but it must become an
event before the release. Give the speaker an office within the manifested law
and demonstrate that office through action, witness, or final tableau. Once the
governing claim is clear, stop restating it in polished variations and spend the
remaining prose on one sustained transformation of the stage. Show at least one
commanded change occurring onstage and end with a tableau that proves the old
canonical claim can no longer stand.

Do not force Latin, Greek, Japanese, or a second release line. Use a single
release when it already names the law and the final impossible image.
Do not include commentary.
```

### 11.10 Structural Critic

```text
Review the invocation for premise, action, source confrontation, spectacle,
and release.

Do not rewrite it yet.

Identify the living line, Commission Invariant drift, source furniture or
disappearance, weak agency, missing or merely announced metaphysical office,
imported spectacle, unanswered commands, governing claim repeated instead of
transformed, promised rather than performed victory, ornate argument, release
failure or forced architecture, protected material, and the smallest decisive
correction. State whether the source's governing word has become a staged
transformation.

State whether the speaker inhabits or usurps the canonical world, or merely
lectures the historical author from outside it. Author address passes only when
authorship itself is the charged conflict.

Verify that the final stage is physically different after the rite and that the
final tableau proves the victory. Do not demand moral health or more cultural
detail merely to display research.
```

### 11.11 Register Critic

```text
Evaluate the invocation only for rhetorical register.

Do not revise it yet.

The target is the protected Register Packet, not polished contemporary dramatic
English.

Identify:

1. every place the syntax becomes too clean, brief, balanced, or modern;
2. every stock cold-villain beat or isolated one-word verdict;
3. every sentence that sounds like elevated narrative prose rather than rite;
4. every chain of balanced slogans written for quotability rather than ritual motion;
5. where the speaker could apostrophize an authorized dramatic target, preen,
   self-coronate, or luxuriate;
6. where the speaker sounds like a modern critic explaining the work rather
   than an inhabitant changing it;
7. whether the historical author is addressed without authorship being central;
8. every image whose excess is generic rather than particular;
9. where the source's rhetorical temperature has been flattened;
10. whether legal, scholarly, theological, or philosophical language remains an
   ornate brief instead of becoming invocation and event;
11. where more density would create intoxication;
12. where density has become empty accumulation;
13. the three passages most in need of a Baroque Pass;
14. lines and irregularities that must remain unchanged.

Do not praise smoothness. Do not demand restraint merely for respectability.
Do not rewrite.
```

### 11.12 Baroque Editor

```text
Perform a source-responsive Baroque Pass on the invocation.

- ceremonial abundance;
- apostrophe to authorized figures, laws, objects, places, events, and
  authorities within the canonical world;

  event;

- visible fulfillment of commands and a final tableau proving victory;
- sensuous attention to established images;
- the speaker's delight in their own authority;
- flamboyance that risks secondhand embarrassment through commitment.

Replace modern minimalist authority beats with fuller, character-specific acts
of contempt, judgment, ecstasy, or self-coronation.

Do not merely add adjectives or stock cosmic imagery. Do not introduce new
symbolic systems. Do not smooth the result into contemporary literary English.
```

### 11.14 Integrity Reviewer

```text

- ornate argument that never becomes an event;

- commands that remain promises rather than stage action;
- a speaker whose only authority is being intellectually correct;
- a metaphysical office announced by title but never enacted;
- generic maximalist filler, imported spectacle, or repeated empty intensifiers;
- the famous dead author used as a portable opponent;

- a final victory visible only in explanation;
```

## 12. Review Readiness and Adoption Authority

Readiness for review is not adoption.

### 12.1 Desire Review Readiness

The field contains 12–16 short, genuinely distinct desires with visible victories and no premature lore.

### 12.2 Canonical Review Readiness

The field contains ten exact, verified, pre-1900 source confrontations with the default Western/Japanese distribution,
unless the Commission justified another field. Each card identifies a dramatic locus inside the source rather than
defaulting to its historical author.

### 12.3 Premise Review Readiness

Eight thumbnails preserve the invariant, source confrontation, and decisive victory without becoming miniature essays.

### 12.4 Voice-Sketch Review Readiness

Six sketches test distinct rhetorical temperatures, dramatic relationships to the canonical world,
self-mythologizing voices, spectacle movements, and release seeds. Author address appears only when explicitly
authorized by the source packet.

### 12.5 Full-Draft Review Readiness

Two finished invocations preserve the same approved premise while offering genuinely different structures.

### 12.6 Human Adoption

In the primary form, only the Human Reviewer grants adoption.

The human may adopt a candidate even when the Operator remains uncertain, or reject one that passes every formal test.

### 12.7 Fully Agent Adoption

In the fully agent form, adoption requires:

- independent selection;
- the Architecture's minimum standard;
- a concise adoption record;
- preservation of the final source and invariant packets.

## Final Canon

The Conclave exists to spend attention where attention is valuable.

Do not spend two hundred words on a desire the human would reject in twenty.

Do not build a civilization around a source that has not been selected.

Do not write the full aria before its rhetorical temperature, accusation, spectacle, and release have survived in
miniature.

The Operator opens the field.

The Human Reviewer chooses what deserves another form.

The Canonical Scout brings old thrones, not antique furniture.

The Premise Builder gives the desire a body.

The Register Designer defines the temperature.

The Ritual Sketcher discovers how that temperature speaks.

The Ritual Writer gives it duration.

The Structural Critic protects it from counterfeit profundity.

The Register Critic exposes modern smoothing.

The Integrator chooses one body and refuses the smooth corpse of consensus.

The Baroque Editor gives the English ceremonial abundance.

The Poetic Editor gives that abundance image, breath, and a blade.

The Integrity Reviewer cuts only counterfeit excess.

Begin from the canon unless the work deliberately refuses it.

Prefer the exact scene to the broad tradition.

Prefer the old prohibition to the invented mechanism.

Let the source remain visible enough to be challenged.

Then let the new speaker enter the canonical world and do something audacious enough to deserve the confrontation.

Seize the myth, not the bibliography. Address the author only when authorship itself is the battlefield.

Match the source's rhetorical temperature and raise it until the new voice can seize the throne without sounding like
polished modern dramatic prose.

> **Generate broadly at low resolution.  
> Spend prose only after selection.**

And when one voice has survived every gate, let it speak the True Name.
